# The 60-Second Call Script (human lane)

For phone-only leads. Same brain the cortese-voice AI uses — tuned for a real
conversation with an owner between lunch and dinner rush. Target length: 45–75
seconds. Goal: **permission to text the demo link.** Not a sale. A demo.

---

## The script

> **"Hi — is this the owner, or whoever handles the phones?** *(gatekeeper beat)*
>
> **My name's Stephen Blanford, I'm local — Binghamton. I'll be quick, I know it's the rush.**
>
> **Reason I'm calling: your phone line. When it's busy on a Friday, that caller doesn't leave a message — they just call the next shop. I built something that fixes it: the second your line goes busy, the caller gets an automatic text — 'sorry we missed you, want to text your order?' — and it lands right on a counter tablet.**
>
> **No new number, no app, nothing for your staff to learn. It costs zero to start — I only bill 15% of the orders it actually saves. No saved orders, no charge, ever.**
>
> **Can I text you the link? It's a 30-second demo — you'll see exactly how it works."**

---

## If they say...

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| **"How much?"** | "Zero upfront, zero monthly. I bill 15 cents on the recovered dollar — only when an order comes back through it. If it saves nothing, you pay nothing." |
| **"We already have online ordering."** | "This isn't online ordering — it's the people who call and get the busy signal. Those folks never see your website. This catches them in the ten seconds they'd otherwise be gone." |
| **"We're too busy / call later."** | "That's exactly when it earns — I'll text the link, you watch it whenever. If it's not obvious in 30 seconds, delete it." *(Text immediately, log the callback day.)* |
| **"Is this a robo-call?"** | "Ha — no, it's me, one guy in Binghamton. I get why you ask. Everything I do has a human on it." |
| **"Not interested."** | "Totally fair. I'll text the link anyway — zero obligation, and if Friday's rush ever eats a caller, you'll know where to find it. Have a great service." *(Text anyway. Log it.)* |
| **"Send me info / email me."** | "Done — what's the best email?" *(Send proposal + demo link within 5 minutes. Log it.)* |
| **Gatekeeper: "He's busy."** | "Of course — I'll text the link to this number. He can watch it in 30 seconds between tickets. What's a good day to follow up?" |

## The five rules

1. **Permission first, pitch second.** Every sentence ends toward "can I text you the link?"
2. **Never argue.** One objection answer, then back to the text ask or a graceful exit.
3. **Text the demo IMMEDIATELY** after any non-no conversation — within 60 seconds, while you're still on their mind.
4. **Log every call** in the lead tracker: name reached, outcome (demo-texted / callback-day / not-interested / gatekeeper), next action.
5. **Best windows:** 2:30–4:30 pm Tue–Thu (between lunch and dinner). Never during Friday rush — you of all people know what that sounds like.

## Why this script works

- **Local in sentence two** — Binghamton means you're a neighbor, not a vendor.
- **Their pain in sentence three** — busy-signal losses, described from their side of the counter.
- **The ledger math in one breath** — 15% of recovered only. The risk story is fully inverted: *they* can't lose money on this.
- **"Text you the link" is a micro-yes** — tiny ask, huge conversion. The demo does the selling.
